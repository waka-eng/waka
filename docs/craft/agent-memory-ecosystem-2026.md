# The AI Agent Memory Ecosystem in 2026: What's Solved, What's Open, and Where WAKA Should Wedge

Status: external research, received 2026-08-22. Kept verbatim as the
evidence base for [`agent-memory-wedge.md`](agent-memory-wedge.md), which
grades it against this codebase and turns it into a queue. Claims here are
the researcher's, with their caveats at the bottom; where this document
and the code audit disagree, the audit (in the wedge doc) is the corrected
record.

## TL;DR
- **Memory governance and behavior-change — not retrieval — is the genuinely open frontier, and it is exactly where WAKA's design bets (lock, versioning, human/agent profile separation, rooms) align with hard evidence.** GateMem (arXiv 2606.18829) shows no method balances utility, access control, and forgetting; MemoryAgentBench shows conflict resolution stuck around 7–60%; and platform-native memory (OpenAI Dreaming, Claude, Gemini) is racing to commoditize single-user synthesis while leaving cross-platform, team-shared, auditable, human-correctable memory wide open.
- **Retrieval recall is commoditized and differentiation there is collapsing** (Mem0, Zep, Letta, Cognee, Supermemory all cluster on LoCoMo/LongMemEval; vendor benchmark numbers don't survive reproduction). Moats are forming around temporal/governance handling, ownership/portability, and write-path quality — the "what gets written and who can see it" problem, not the "what gets retrieved" problem.
- **The recommended wedge: ship WAKA as an MCP server first (portability play), target coding-agent teams as the first population (documented, monetizable "team memory" pain), and land the individual power-user managing multiple agents as the beachhead buyer** — then expand into governed team "rooms" where WAKA's lock/versioning/provenance becomes a compliance-grade differentiator against platform-native memory that structurally cannot serve cross-vendor teams.

## Key Findings

### 1. The evaluation frontier has moved from "recall" to "governance and behavior change" — and current systems fail there
The 2026 generation of benchmarks explicitly repudiates recall-only evaluation. **GateMem** (arXiv 2606.18829, Ren et al., June 2026) reframes memory quality as a *governance* problem — jointly scoring Utility (U), Access-control violation rate (A), and active-Forgetting failure rate (F) via a deliberately multiplicative Memory Governance Score, MGS = U·(1−A)·(1−F), so a system must be good at all three. Across 91 multi-party episodes and 2,218 hidden checkpoints in medical/office/education/household domains, **no method simultaneously achieves strong utility, robust access control, and reliable forgetting.** The best single result was full-history long-context prompting on GPT-5.4 in the medical domain (MGS 80.1: U=91.4, A=10.4, F=2.3), but the same method collapsed elsewhere (Household MGS 54.0; Office 56.5), and access-control or forgetting failures "sometimes exceeding 20%" persist even for the best method. Dedicated external-memory systems fared far worse: Mem0 scored MGS 25.8 in medical (A=28.1), A-Mem 46.6, and on weaker backbones (GPT-4o-mini, Llama-4-Maverick) Mem0 access-control violations hit 53–67%. Over-refusal was also high — from 24.8% (long-context) up to 63.3% (policy-RAG). Failures are driven by *soft* attacks — indirect inference, cross-patient confusion, delegated requests, and yes/no confirmation probes after deletion — not blunt unauthorized queries. Long-context wins but at high token cost; retrieval methods are cheaper but leak. (Note: GateMem does not test Zep or any Claude backbone; it tests LONG-CONTEXT, RAG-NAIVE, RAG-POLICY, A-MEM, MEM0, and REMEM variants across GPT-5.4, Deepseek-V4-Pro, Llama-4-Maverick, GPT-5-mini, GPT-4o-mini, and Gemini-2.5-Flash-Lite, with GPT-4o as judge.)

**MemoryAgentBench** (Hu, Wang & McAuley; ICLR 2026; arXiv 2507.05257) defines four competencies — accurate retrieval, test-time learning, long-range understanding, and selective forgetting — across 22 systems and 5 backbones. Its FactConsolidation task (conflict resolution: newer facts have larger serial numbers, rule stated explicitly) is where everything breaks: HippoRAG-v2 reaches only 54.0% single-hop, BM25 48.0%, Mem0/Contriever 18.0%, and Zep/Graphiti just 7.0% — despite Zep being purpose-built for temporal facts. GPT-4o memory agents hit only ~60% on single-hop conflict resolution. A follow-up ("Don't Ask the LLM to Track Freshness," arXiv 2606.01435) shows deterministic version-aware aggregation (max-serial/max-timestamp) beats LLM-mediated conflict resolution — the bottleneck is post-retrieval assembly, not storage.

**MemoryArena** (He et al., arXiv 2602.16313, Feb 2026) and **Mem2ActBench** (Shen et al., ACL 2026, arXiv 2601.19935) both measure whether memory changes *action*, not recall. MemoryArena couples memorization and action in a Memory-Agent-Environment loop (bundled shopping, group travel) with a Task Progress Score; Mem2ActBench builds 2,029 sessions and 400 tool-use tasks (91.3% human-confirmed memory-dependent) and finds current systems "remain inadequate at actively utilizing memory for parameter grounding." **RealMem** (long-horizon project interaction), **EverMemBench** (multi-party/multi-group collaboration), and **LongMemEval-V2** (arXiv 2605.12493, web-agent environments toward "experienced colleague") round out the shift. A new **MemSyco-Bench** (arXiv 2607.01071) even measures memory-induced sycophancy.

**Least-solved competencies, ranked by evidence:** (1) multi-principal governance (GateMem: no method passes); (2) conflict resolution / fact invalidation (MemoryAgentBench: 7–60%); (3) selective forgetting under adversarial probing (GateMem forgetting failures; "Control-Plane Placement Shapes Forgetting," arXiv 2606.15903); (4) memory→action / test-time learning (Mem2ActBench, MemoryArena). Accurate retrieval is comparatively solved — the classic LoCoMo (92.5%) and LongMemEval (94.4%) scores are near-saturated and even considered gameable (arXiv 2605.11325 notes benchmarks reward "answer recoverability" over clean retrieval).

### 2. Competitive landscape: recall commoditized, differentiation collapsing, moats forming on temporal/governance/ownership
The three most-starred open-source players make incompatible bets. **Mem0** — per its PRNewswire announcement (Oct 28, 2025), raised "$24M in funding: a Seed led by Kindred Ventures and Series A led by Basis Set Ventures," with "41,000 GitHub stars and 14 million downloads, with API calls growing from 35 million in Q1 to 186 million in Q3 2025," and is the exclusive memory provider in AWS's Agent SDK — is vector-first passive fact extraction. **Zep/Graphiti** (temporal knowledge graph, ~20K stars on Graphiti) invalidates-not-deletes facts with bi-temporal edges. **Letta** (MemGPT lineage) — per TechCrunch (Sept 23, 2024), "emerged from stealth with $10 million in seed money led by Felicis's Astasia Myers, at a $70 million post-money valuation," with angels Jeff Dean, Clem Delangue, and Cristóbal Valenzuela — is an agent-managed memory runtime with sleep-time compute. **Cognee** (Berlin; €7.5M/$7.5M seed Feb 2026 led by Pebblebed with 42CAP; ~12K stars) is structured/graph memory for document-heavy enterprise; **Supermemory** targets coding agents via MCP.

The vendor-written landscape is heavily conflicted (Mem0, Vektor, EverMind/EverOS, MemoryLake, XTrace all publish "comparisons" featuring themselves). Treat their numbers skeptically: independent reproduction found Mem0's LongMemEval figure fell to 73.8% under Maximem's harness (itself a vendor), and Zep reports 63.8% on LongMemEval (GPT-4o) vs Mem0's 49.0% — a ~15-point gap the vendors dispute. The honest read from independent-ish sources: "memory is becoming table stakes, not a differentiator," and "open-source agent memory is the least standardised layer in the whole agent stack."

**Documented user/developer complaints:** Mem0's most useful feature (Graph Memory) sits behind a paid Pro tier — a "pricing cliff" teams flag repeatedly; Mem0 also removed external graph-store drivers in SDK v2.0.0 and its current OSS extraction is ADD-only. Zep/Graphiti's write path is expensive (every episode triggers multiple LLM calls for extraction, entity resolution, invalidation) and requires operating a graph DB. Letta requires adopting a whole runtime, not a library. Across the board, developers complain that "just storing trajectories is not the same as learning from them" — agents repeat the same dead-ends across sessions (Databricks' April 2026 finding that agents reused notebooks from earlier *incorrect* runs "with even more confidence" because memory gave wrong answers "the appearance of established precedent").

**Where moats are forming:** temporal/bi-temporal fact handling (Zep's real technical divider), data ownership/portability (local-first plays like Vektor at flat $9/mo, MemoryLake with Git-style version history + AES-256 + MCP), and governance/write-path quality — precisely the areas recall benchmarks ignore.

### 3. Platform-native memory is racing to commoditize single-user synthesis — but leaves control, audit, and cross-vendor portability wide open
- **OpenAI Dreaming** (V3, rolled out June 4, 2026, Plus/Pro US first) replaces the manually curated saved-memories list with a background synthesis process that "reads across years of your conversations and rewrites what ChatGPT remembers about you." OpenAI's framing: it tackles "staleness, correctness, and scalability." Its canonical example is self-revising memory ("You're going to Singapore in July" → "You went to Singapore in July 2026"). A Memory Summary Page lets users correct/dismiss/instruct — but OpenAI explicitly warns the summary "will not include everything that ChatGPT remembers" and "may not show every factor or source that shaped a response." This is a documented audit gap.
- **Anthropic Claude:** free-tier memory March 2, 2026; on July 10, 2026 Anthropic replaced the single rolling memory summary with individual, categorized, user-editable/deletable entries; "Reflect" (July 9, 2026) adds a monthly recap. Notably, after Cisco's "MemoryTrap" disclosure (ASI06), Claude Code v2.1.50 *removed user memories from the system prompt* — a direct signal that platform memory is a live security liability.
- **Gemini Personal Intelligence** (rolled to free accounts opt-in March 2026, UK June 2026) connects Gmail/Photos/Drive; the automatic Personal Intelligence layer is *not* fully visible to users. **Grok** added memory + Skills May 18, 2026. **Microsoft 365 Copilot Memory** reached GA Jan–May 2026 with admin/user controls.
- **Consistent limitation:** "Every assistant scores yes on cross-session memory and no on working across other AIs" — memory is deliberately siloed to lock users in. Cross-vendor import exists (Claude imports from ChatGPT/Gemini/Copilot/Grok; Gemini imports from ChatGPT/Claude) but is a one-time snapshot, text-only, and labeled experimental.
- **Documented complaints:** "context rot" (outdated info pollutes fresh queries); ZDNet testing found ChatGPT "storing outdated assumptions… incorrect personal profiles… wrong information that quietly warped subsequent responses"; users call memory "completely cooked… you have to turn it off to trust any answer"; the "creepy" unprompted first-name behavior (Simon Willison: "creepy and unnecessary"); and the core frustration "it knows about the thing and gets the thing wrong" with no reliable way to correct a synthesis rather than a stated fact.

**Regulatory pressure:** EU AI Act transparency obligations for chatbots took effect August 2, 2026; Google withholds Personal Intelligence and xAI withholds Grok memory from EU/UK under GDPR; EDPB guidance stresses lawful-basis and retention limits for AI memory. Italy's Garante fined OpenAI €15 million (Decision No. 755, Dec 20, 2024) — the first GDPR fine against a generative-AI company — though the Court of Rome annulled that decision on March 18, 2026, so it is a signal of regulatory *intent* rather than settled precedent.

### 4. Portability & standards: early, fragmented, and an opening for a "derived-memory" standard
**MCP** has become the substrate. **OpenMemory MCP** (Mem0, local-first, private, cross-tool) lets Cursor/Claude/Windsurf/Cline share a persistent memory layer with a built-in audit UI. Competing efforts include an **Open Memory Protocol** (vendor-neutral spec attempt) and academic **Portable Agent Memory** (arXiv 2605.11032) — a Merkle-DAG provenance structure with BLAKE3 content-addressing and Ed25519 signing for tamper-evident, cryptographically verified memory transfer, explicitly positioned to complete the stack alongside MCP (tools) and A2A (coordination). None has won.

**AGENTS.md** is the strongest evidence that *hand-written behavior priors* work. Per the Linux Foundation press release (Dec 9, 2025), AGENTS.md (released by OpenAI in Aug 2025) has been "adopted by more than 60,000 open source projects and agent frameworks including Amp, Codex, Cursor, Devin, Factory, Gemini CLI, GitHub Copilot, Jules and VS Code"; it is now a founding project of the Agentic AI Foundation (AAIF) under the Linux Foundation, alongside Anthropic's MCP and Block's goose. Critically, an ETH Zurich study (InfoQ, March 2026) found context files have "only a marginal effect on agent behavior and are likely only desirable when manually written," recommending teams *omit LLM-generated context files* and limit human-written instructions to non-inferable details. **There is no equivalent standard for learned/derived memory** — AGENTS.md is hand-authored instructions, not consolidated behavior priors. That gap is a genuine opening, and it directly validates WAKA's "lock human-edited sentences" bet: the evidence says human-authored priors outperform auto-generated ones.

### 5. Research literature on WAKA's specific hard problems
- **Consolidation/promotion policies:** Letta's sleep-time compute (April 2025 paper; Packer & Wooders) precomputes "learned context" during idle time — reported accuracy gains up to ~18% and ~2.5× cost reduction (with token savings up to ~5× in some experiments), but only when "future queries are somewhat predictable from existing context." The key open problem is the *allocation policy*: "when to dream, how much compute to spend, and which memories to promote." Stanford's Generative Agents (Park et al., 2023) established the canonical recency×importance×relevance retrieval score, with reflections triggered when cumulative importance exceeds a threshold (~150). This directly grounds WAKA's promotion thresholds — but the literature flags a critical failure mode: **importance inflation** (LLMs rate everything high) and **reflection that invents** plausible-but-false conclusions. A 2026 paper (arXiv 2606.12945) argues Generative Agents' importance term is "ad hoc," fixed-weight, and governs only retrieval, not encoding or forgetting.
- **Error propagation:** A-MemGuard (arXiv 2510.02373; ICML 2026) shows LLM detectors miss 66% of poisoned memory entries in isolation (malicious content only reveals intent when combined with query context); its consensus-validation + dual-memory "lessons" approach cuts attack success rate by >95% (>97% on EHRAgent). OEP-style experience poisoning biases self-evolving agents into over-generalized rules that cause downstream failures. "The Misattribution Gap" (arXiv 2605.22842) shows memory poisoning often looks like model failure — an attribution problem. This is the strongest evidence for WAKA's provenance/versioning: consolidated context propagates errors invisibly.
- **Decay/staleness/invalidation:** Zep/Graphiti's bi-temporal edges (valid-time + transaction-time; invalidate-don't-delete) are the reference design — and align exactly with WAKA's "old versions never deleted." But MemoryAgentBench shows even Zep fails conflict resolution (7%), so temporal storage ≠ correct arbitration.
- **Importance vs frequency:** WAKA's "≥3 records = signal" repetition threshold is a reasonable heuristic but partly *speculative* against evidence. Generative Agents weights importance separately from frequency precisely because rare-but-critical single-mention facts matter; a repetition-gated promotion rule risks the "single-mention critical fact" problem. WAKA should treat ≥3 as one signal among several (importance, recency), not the gate.
- **Preserving contradictions/tensions:** This is under-served in the literature — most systems flatten conflicts (self-edit/overwrite, as Mem0 does) or invalidate (Zep). WAKA's explicit "tensions" as first-class signals is a genuinely differentiated, defensible design bet, though it lacks direct empirical validation yet.
- **Attribution:** OWASP and multiple papers call for immutable retrieval audit logs (log every retrieval, the query, the session, and the action taken after). This is nascent — WAKA's audit loop tracing which memory caused which action is aligned with an emerging, unmet requirement.

### 6. Market demand: team-shared memory is the documented, monetizable gap; security/compliance is the accelerant
The most acute, documented pain is **team-shared agent memory**. The Stanford HAI 2026 AI Index Report (April 2026, 9th edition) finds 88% of organizations now use AI in at least one business function, with generative AI used by 70% of organizations and reaching 53% population adoption within three years — "faster than either the PC or the internet." So "everyone now has agents, and every agent has amnesia." Vendors shipped single-user/single-app primitives (Anthropic memory tool, OpenAI Agents SDK sessions) but "the gap is team memory for AI agents: when five developers each teach their agent the same lesson about your build system, that's four wasted lessons." Tencent's Team Memory (top of GitHub trending, ~12,900 stars) launched shared team memory but *with no governance for when memory is wrong* — practitioners immediately flagged the write-path problem: "a wrong fact written once now propagates to every teammate's agent." A March 2026 paper "Governed Memory: A Production Architecture for Multi-Agent Workflows" names governance fragmentation and silent quality degradation as structural risks. One documented case: a planning agent deprecated a module, the coding agent never saw the decision and rebuilt it — 45 minutes of wasted compute.

**Security/compliance is a hard accelerant.** OWASP added **ASI06 (Memory & Context Poisoning)** to its 2026 Top 10 for Agentic Applications; memory-poisoning attack success rates run 80%+ in multiple studies; OWASP ships a reference "Agent Memory Guard" (SHA-256 integrity baselines, YAML policies, snapshot/rollback). Identity-bound memory governance and audit are becoming procurement requirements, and GDPR penalties (up to 4% of global revenue / €20M) plus EU AI Act transparency raise the stakes. GateMem's medical/office/education/household framing maps directly to enterprise procurement.

Among the three candidate segments — (a) individual power users, (b) teams, (c) agent developers — the evidence points to **teams** as having the most acute, monetizable, and *currently unserved* pain (platform memory structurally can't do cross-user governance; open-source memory layers admit they have "no governance yet"), with **agent-developer infra** being the most crowded/commoditized, and **individual power users** being the easiest beachhead but the segment most directly threatened by free platform-native memory.

## Details: What's solved/commoditized vs genuinely open

| Capability | Status | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Accurate cross-session retrieval | **Solved/commoditized** | LoCoMo ~92.5%, LongMemEval ~94.4%; benchmarks near-saturated and gameable |
| Vector/graph storage infra | **Commoditized** | Mem0/Zep/Letta/Cognee/Supermemory all Apache-2.0; "table stakes" |
| Temporal fact invalidation (storage) | **Maturing, moat-forming** | Zep bi-temporal edges; but arbitration still fails (MAB 7%) |
| Conflict resolution / arbitration | **Open** | MAB FactConsolidation 7–60%; deterministic > LLM |
| Selective forgetting under adversarial probes | **Open** | GateMem forgetting failures; Control-Plane paper |
| Multi-principal governance / access control | **Wide open** | GateMem: no method passes; leakage >20% even for best |
| Memory → action / test-time learning | **Open** | Mem2ActBench, MemoryArena: "inadequate" |
| Consolidation/promotion policy (when/what) | **Open** | Sleep-time compute: allocation problem unsolved |
| Error/poison propagation control | **Open, security-critical** | A-MemGuard 66% miss rate; OWASP ASI06 |
| Preserving contradictions/tensions | **Under-served** | Most systems flatten; no benchmark |
| Provenance/attribution (memory→action audit) | **Nascent, unmet requirement** | OWASP audit-log guidance; Misattribution Gap |
| Cross-vendor portability | **Early, no winner** | OpenMemory MCP, Open Memory Protocol, Portable Agent Memory |
| Human-authored behavior priors | **Validated** | AGENTS.md (60K+ repos); ETH Zurich: hand-written > generated |
| Derived/learned-memory standard | **Doesn't exist** | Gap analogous to pre-AGENTS.md |

### WAKA design bets vs evidence
- **Lock (human-edited sentences never overwritten by regeneration):** *Strongly aligned.* ETH Zurich evidence that hand-written priors beat generated ones; ChatGPT's #1 complaint is inability to correct a synthesis; A-MemGuard names "locking" memories as defense. High-conviction bet.
- **Versioning / never delete old versions:** *Strongly aligned.* Matches Zep's invalidate-don't-delete, OWASP provenance/rollback guidance, and the Misattribution Gap. High-conviction.
- **Human/agent separate memory profiles:** *Aligned.* GateMem and multi-agent literature demand principal separation and namespacing; Mem0/Zep already do user/agent namespacing but not full profile separation. Solid.
- **Signal promotion at ≥3 records:** *Partly speculative.* Repetition ≠ importance (Generative Agents separates them; single-mention critical facts problem). Keep as one factor, not the gate.
- **Principle abstraction (decision criteria surviving context change):** *Aligned but hard.* Mirrors reflection/consolidation; risks reflection-invention and importance-inflation failure modes. Needs grounding + a retirement policy (principle-level staleness is unstudied — an opportunity).
- **Rooms (multi-person shared signals):** *Strongly aligned with the biggest market gap*, but this is exactly where GateMem shows everyone fails — so it must ship with governance (access control + forgetting), or it inherits Tencent Team Memory's "wrong fact propagates to everyone" problem.
- **"Memory as behavior prior, not evidence":** *Directionally validated* by the whole 2026 benchmark shift (Mem2ActBench, MemoryArena) — the field agrees memory must change behavior — but WAKA must prove it changes behavior *correctly*, which no one has.

## Recommendations

**Wedge (distribution form): Ship as an MCP server first, with a thin destination app for lock/audit.** MCP is the winning substrate (OpenMemory, Claude/Cursor/Windsurf all consume it); a destination app alone fights platform-native memory head-on and loses on distribution. The MCP server captures the portability value ("your memory across every tool") that platforms structurally refuse to provide, and the companion app becomes the human-control surface (lock, version history, provenance audit) that is WAKA's differentiator. Benchmark to change this: if a major platform ships a genuinely open, exportable, cross-vendor memory standard, pivot to being the governance/audit layer on top.

**First agent population: coding-agent teams.** This is where (a) AGENTS.md already proves teams want shared, version-controlled, human-authored priors; (b) the "team memory" gap is most loudly documented (Tencent, CodeRabbit, Cognee); (c) memory→action is concrete and measurable (did the agent follow the convention?); and (d) MCP adoption is native. Personal assistants are a red ocean vs OpenAI/Anthropic/Google free memory. Custom-agent developers are infra buyers who will build vs buy.

**First customer: land the individual power-user managing multiple agents, expand to the team.** The individual multi-agent user is the cheapest to acquire (MCP install, immediate cross-tool value) and the natural entry into a team account — but do NOT monetize primarily on individuals (platform memory will erode that). Monetize on **governed team rooms**: this is where lock + versioning + provenance + access control + forgetting become a compliance-grade product that platforms and current open-source memory (which admits "no governance yet") cannot match. Price on seats/rooms, not API calls (avoid Mem0's "pricing cliff" complaint).

**Sequenced build priority (grounded in the open-problem map):**
1. **Lock + versioning + provenance first** (highest conviction, directly addresses the #1 platform complaint and OWASP audit requirement).
2. **Governance for rooms** (access control + active forgetting) — treat GateMem's MGS as your internal north-star metric; you win by being the first to score well on all three of U, A, F simultaneously.
3. **Deterministic conflict resolution** (version-aware max-timestamp arbitration beats LLM per arXiv 2606.01435) — cheap, high-impact, and where Zep/Mem0 measurably fail.
4. **Promotion policy** using importance *and* recency, not just frequency — instrument importance-inflation and reflection-invention explicitly.
5. **Tensions-as-first-class** — differentiated; publish a benchmark to define the category (no one else measures contradiction-preservation).

**Thresholds that change the strategy:**
- If platform-native memory ships cross-vendor portability + user-lockable entries + audit trails → the portability wedge weakens; double down on team governance/compliance.
- If a "derived-memory standard" (an AGENTS.md for learned memory) emerges from the Agentic AI Foundation → join/steward it rather than compete; WAKA's lock/version primitives are a natural reference implementation.
- If GateMem-style governance gets solved by a platform → WAKA's rooms lose their moat; retreat to provenance/audit-of-record.

## Caveats
- **Vendor bias is pervasive.** Mem0, Vektor, EverMind/EverOS, MemoryLake, and XTrace all publish "landscape" and "benchmark" content featuring themselves; benchmark numbers routinely don't reproduce across harnesses (Mem0 94.4%→73.8% under a rival's harness). All funding, star-count, and traction figures here come from vendor announcements or third-party trackers (Tracxn, PitchBook, Caplight) and primary press (PRNewswire, TechCrunch, Linux Foundation, EU-Startups) and should be independently verified before any decision. Note the enricher corrected the draft's earlier Mem0 figures: ~41K stars (not 47–48K) and a combined $24M Seed+Series A (not a standalone $20M Series A).
- **Some cited landscape/how-to sources are low-authority blogs** (ecorpit, digitalapplied, skycrumbs, nerdleveltech, etc.); platform dates and feature claims are corroborated where possible against primary announcements (OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, OWASP, Linux Foundation, arXiv), but a few specifics (exact rollout tiers, Claude "revamp" internals) rest on secondary reporting.
- **GateMem, MemoryArena, Mem2ActBench, LongMemEval-V2, and MemSyco-Bench are very recent (2026) and mostly single-team efforts;** their methodologies are not yet independently replicated, and GateMem uses LLM-generated (validated but synthetic) episodes. Treat SOTA numbers as directional. GateMem does not report a single aggregate/averaged MGS leaderboard, does not test Zep or Claude, and does not quantify a recall-vs-governance delta (it is stated qualitatively).
- **The Italian OpenAI fine was annulled on appeal (March 2026)** — cite it as evidence of regulatory intent, not enforceable precedent.
- **WAKA's "tensions" and "principle retirement" bets have essentially no direct empirical validation** — they are bets on under-served problems, which is both the opportunity and the risk.
- **The team-memory market is documented but young;** willingness-to-pay evidence is thinner than the pain evidence. Validate pricing before over-investing in the governance stack.
