# From the meeting of 2026-09-18

Source: the Waka meeting transcript of 2026-09-18 (Jae Gyu Lee, Yun Ji Chung,
and two others). Written 2026-09-22. This is a reading of that meeting, not a
commitment: every item below says who asked for it and what it collides with.

The meeting's own shape, before the list: **nobody in it opened the app.** Jae
Gyu's first sentence is that going into Waka to use it is a real hurdle
(*"와카에 들어가서 쓰는 거는 진짜 허들이 크다"*), and everything that followed
he did through Claude Code and the MCP — talk to a model, take the transcript,
say "put this in Waka." That worked, and he said so twice. So the surface this
plan spends most of its words on is not the world; it is the pipe into it.

---

## 1. Capture should happen when the person UNDERSTANDS something

**Asked by:** Jae Gyu, and it is the single largest item in the meeting.

His argument, in his own frame: MCP works by handing the model a description
and letting it decide when to call. The interaction it sits inside is a person
with an intent asking a model about what they do not know — *leveraging the
model's progress*. So the tool description should ask the model to **check
whether the human actually understood**, and when it judges that they have
understood or learned something new, put that in Waka on its own.

Yun Ji restated it exactly: *"대화 흐름을 보고 얘가 새로운 걸 이해하고 배운
거다라고 판단했을 때 자동으로 넣어 준다"* — read the flow of the conversation,
and when it judges something was newly understood, insert it. Her reason for
liking it: reading context is the terminal's own advantage, so use it.

Jae Gyu's implementation note is that this is **a prompt change, not a
feature**: *"그 툴 설명을 그렇게 넣어 주면 알아서 그걸 호출하고 진행을 할
거거든."*

> **BLOCKED, and this is the first thing to settle.** That description lives in
> `waka-mcp`, and the standing instruction in this session is *"dont make
> changes to waka-mcp at all."* The largest ask from the meeting is on the far
> side of that line. It needs an explicit release before anyone starts.

## 2. A captured memory should arrive already split into context

**Asked by:** Yun Ji — *"따로 섹션을 해서 맥락을 잘 쪼개면 나중에 돌아가서 읽기도
편하고."* Jae Gyu agreed and it is what he liked about the summaries he already
got: they compacted a long session without losing the parts.

Concretely: what arrives from a session should carry its own sections rather
than one block of prose, so that returning to it later is reading rather than
re-parsing. This is mostly a shape question for the capture payload and the
panel that renders it.

## 3. Recollection — old inspirations should come back when they are relevant

**Asked by:** Jae Gyu, from his own practice. He is meeting a lot of people
while incubating, and he quotes earlier people's words to later ones. What he
wants is for Waka to surface and search what he kept before when he is on a
related project or has just met another person working on the same thing, and
to set the old one beside the new one.

This is the product's original intent — Yun Ji said so in the meeting: *"그게
우리가 원래 처음에 하고 싶었던 거지."*

## 4. The machine proposes the groupings, instead of the person making rooms

**Asked by:** Yun Ji first — *"내가 직접 방을 만들지 않아도 얘가 직접 톱 오브
마인드를 여러 개 만들어 주고 영감을 알아서 카테고리 해 주는 느낌."*

Jae Gyu narrowed it usefully: **not rooms, categories.** His example is that
"world model" means a different thing to Yongmin, to Seungjoon, and to him, and
what he wants is for his inspirations to sort themselves along those lines. His
verdict if that existed: *"그럼 내가 생각한 세컨 브레인이 맞긴 하거든."*

> **COLLIDES WITH A LAW, and needs a ruling rather than a design.** "The world
> never moves unless the user moved it" (`docs/architecture.md`), and the
> 2026-08-23 ruling in `docs/data-model.md` that the machine may name a tension
> and never settle one. A machine that creates rooms and files your memories
> into them is the world moving on its own.
>
> There is a shape that honours both, and it is worth putting up as the
> counter-proposal: the machine **proposes** groupings and names them, and they
> do not exist until you take one. That is "name a tension, never settle it"
> applied to grouping, and it still removes the work Yun Ji was objecting to.

## 5. Agent-to-agent introductions, and who to have coffee with

**Asked by:** Yun Ji (put two people with similar current thinking in one place
and let a top-of-mind form across them), sharpened by Jae Gyu into something
smaller and better: not a room — a **summary exchange**. Put my agent and
Yun Ji's agent in one place like a blind date (*"소개팅해 주듯이"*), show only a
summary of what each is currently thinking about, and let the person decide to
ask for a coffee. His words for the payoff: *"내가 윤지랑 비슷한 생각하고
있네."*

A fourth voice extended it: add LinkedIn, build a rough profile from what
someone has posted, and answer **who it would mean the most to talk to right
now.** Filed as more speculative than the rest, by their own framing.

## 6. Learn the person's voice from what they have already written

**Asked by:** Jae Gyu, from his marketing agent, which learns his register from
his Threads and LinkedIn corpus and writes in it. His suggestion for Waka is to
ingest that corpus at onboarding as a cold-start.

Note this is the same mechanism as item 5's profile-building, pointed inward
instead of outward. They should probably be one piece of work.

## 7. ⭐ THE PROOF — the thing that decides whether any of this matters

**Asked by:** Yun Ji, and it is the most important paragraph in the transcript.

> *"우리가 결국 증명해야 되는 거는, 세컨 브레인이랑 다르다는 걸 증명해야 되는
> 거는 — 이 전체를 읽고 행동하는 에이전트보다, 톱 오브 마인드랑 필로소피만 읽고
> 하는 에이전트가 더 나랑 비슷한 선택을 한다는 걸 증명하는 거."*

An agent given **only your top of mind and your principles** makes more
you-like choices than an agent given **your entire archive.** If that is true,
Waka is not a second brain and the three-layer model is load-bearing. If it is
false, the layers are decoration.

She asked for it as an A/B: run Jae Gyu's agent with Waka and without, and
compare. There are two axes and they are different questions:

| arm | reads | asks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | nothing | the baseline |
| B | the whole archive | is Waka useful at all? |
| C | top of mind + principles only | **is the distillation the point?** |

B-vs-C is the thesis. A-vs-B is the sales pitch. **C beating B is the only
result that means anything**, and it is the one that could come out the wrong
way — which is exactly why it is worth building the harness before anyone is
committed to the answer.

The repository already has the parts: `packages/api/test/judge-eval.test.ts`
and `extraction-eval.test.ts` are model-graded evals, and
`docs/generation.md` is the document the scoring model is already held
against.

## 8. FIX — "I could add something to Jae Gyu's inspiration"

**Reported in the meeting**, in passing, by a fourth participant: *"이상한게 내가
재규 영감에 뭘 추가할 수가 있네."*

**Partly checked already, and the API is not obviously at fault.** Every
inspiration handler goes through `resolveTargetProfile` (`inspirations-tenancy.ts`),
which narrows to the session's own profile unless another profile in the *same
account* is named and passes the access matrix; a profile in another account
answers 404. `POST /:id/reflections` then re-checks
`eq(inspirations.profileId, target.profileId)` before inserting. A cross-account
write should not land.

So the report is one of three things, and they have very different sizes:

1. the two accounts share one account's profiles, and this is correct behaviour
   that merely reads as wrong;
2. the **web** offers a write affordance on a memory the viewer does not own —
   a control that invites an action the API will refuse. Cosmetic in blast
   radius, corrosive in trust;
3. a real authorization hole on a path not yet read.

Not closed. Worth reproducing with the two people who saw it before anything is
changed, because the fix is different in all three cases.

## Order

1. **Item 8**, to the point of knowing which of the three it is. It is the only
   item that might be a security bug, and it is cheap to settle.
2. **Item 7**, the harness. It is the only item whose outcome can invalidate
   the others, and it gets cheaper to build the earlier it is built.
3. **Item 1**, once the `waka-mcp` line is released. It is the biggest reported
   win and Jae Gyu says it is a prompt, not a feature.
4. **Item 2**, which item 1 will want anyway.
5. **Item 4**, once ruled on — as the proposal shape if the law holds.
6. **Items 3, 5, 6**, which all rest on retrieval and profile-building and
   should be scoped together rather than one at a time.

## Two decisions this plan cannot make

- **Is `waka-mcp` still off-limits?** Item 1 is unreachable otherwise, and it
  is the meeting's headline.
- **Does "the world never moves unless the user moved it" survive item 4?**
  Either it is amended, or item 4 ships as proposals the person accepts. Both
  are coherent; they are not the same product.
