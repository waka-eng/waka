# The first-run audit — an account with nothing in it

**Date:** 2026-08-26
**Instrument:** `pnpm e2e:first-sixty` (`tools/e2e/first-sixty.ts`)

## Why this surface had never been measured

Every instrument in this repo walks a world that already has things in it.
`journeys.ts` signs up and immediately keeps five memories. `when-it-fails.ts`
keeps five to have one to delete. `dead-css.ts` makes a room before it can
walk one, and says so plainly:

> *"a fresh account has none, and every selector belonging to a room read as
> dead for exactly that reason."*

All of them treat the empty account as a step to get past. It is the one
screen every single person sees, without exception, before they see any other
— and the product's posture, *no quiet corners*, is at its hardest here,
because an empty world has nothing to show and the easiest thing to render is
nothing.

The two gates whose names sound like they cover this do not:

| gate | what it actually asks |
| --- | --- |
| `e2e:first-paint` | does the world render at all with every CDN blocked? Infrastructure. Says nothing about *what* it renders. |
| `e2e:cold-start` | does the pipeline synthesise a top of mind from four unrelated thoughts? API-level, no browser — and it begins by saving four things, so not empty. |

## The whole product, as a newcomer receives it

Signed up, nothing created. This is the complete visible text of the world:

```
TOP OF MIND
Forming — nothing adopted yet
CAPTURE INSPIRATION
ME   ▾   YOUR ACCOUNT   PHILOSOPHIES & VALUES
```

Seven lines. Six are uppercase labels — which is not a reading of tone but a
fact about the markup: the label register applies `text-transform: uppercase`
to every label class, so an uppercase line **is** a label by construction.

The single line that is not a label describes an absence, and names a concept
(`TOP OF MIND`) the person has never encountered.

## The table

```
the world, just arrived    SAYS       5 controls   "Forming — nothing adopted yet"
the mouth, opened          BARE       9 controls
your own wall's panel      SAYS       7 controls   "Everything you keep, and what WAKA has read out of it."
the walls list             BARE       7 controls   only: ADD NEW
make another place         SAYS       8 controls   "A wall a few of you keep…" / "A mind of its own…"
the timeline               NOT THERE
philosophies & values      SAYS       5 controls   "Your philosophies and values will form here."
your settings              BARE      10 controls
the archive door           NOT THERE
```

**4 say something · 3 show only furniture · 0 blank · 2 do not exist**

`SAYS` means at least one line of prose. Prose is told from furniture by case
plus a word count, with separators stripped — see the classifier's own notes
for the two false SAYS the first run produced.

## Findings

### 1. Nothing on first arrival says what this is for

The empty world's only sentence is about an absence, in a vocabulary the
person has not been given. There is no line anywhere on the first screen that
says what WAKA does, what to put in it, or why.

This is not the same complaint as F4 in the usability audit (*the product
defines none of its own vocabulary*), though they touch. F4 is about words
being undefined. This is about the first screen having no sentence addressed
to a newcomer at all — the defined-ness of the words is a second problem
underneath it.

**Founder's call, deliberately left alone.** The product's voice is not
something an audit gets to invent, and the adjacent surface carries an
explicit ruling (below). A concrete proposal rather than an open question: one
line under `TOP OF MIND`, replacing `Forming — nothing adopted yet` while the
account is empty, saying what will appear there and what makes it appear.
The empty-state prose already written for `philosophies & values` —
*"Your philosophies and values will form here."* — is exactly the shape, and
the top of mind is the one surface of the three that lacks it.

### 2. The mouth is bare by ruling, not by neglect

Opening the mouth gives a box, `PRIVATE / PUBLIC`, `SAVE`, `REFLECT`, and a
placeholder reading `Capture inspiration` — the same two words as the button
just pressed. Nothing says what to write or why.

The code says why, and it is not an oversight:

> `/* the resting phrase, kept — opening the mouth changes nothing about it
> (founder, 2026-08-24) */`

**Not changed.** Recorded here with its cost — a person's first press of the
product's primary control tells them nothing new — so the ruling is visible
next to what it buys and what it costs, rather than being rediscovered as a
bug by the next audit.

### 3. Two of nine surfaces do not exist for an empty account

`the timeline` (`.jar-when`) and `the archive door` (`.jar-saved`) are absent
from the DOM entirely — confirmed by counting elements, not by a selector
timing out. So is `.jar-dial`.

This is defensible and arguably right: *the world never moves unless the user
moved it*, and a timeline of nothing is furniture. It is recorded because the
consequence is easy to miss — a newcomer is given **no evidence that an
archive or a timeline exist**, so the shape of the product cannot be inferred
from its empty state. Whether that is the intended first impression is a
product question, not a defect.

### 4. One dead declaration (fixed)

`.jar-mouth-field::placeholder` was two adjacent blocks on the same selector;
the first's `color: var(--jar-muted)` never applied, overridden on the next
line by `--jar-faint`. Merged. Nothing a person sees changes.

## What the instrument got wrong, twice, before it got it right

Recorded because the table above should be trusted exactly as far as the
probes behind it.

1. **Two false `SAYS` on the first run.** `Forming — nothing adopted yet`
   counted the em dash as a word to reach four. `how it reads you · the
   antechamber` is a row of links joined by a middle dot, read as one
   sentence. Both are furniture; both scored as prose. Separators are stripped
   now, and any line containing `·` is refused outright. This is the expensive
   direction of error for this walk in particular — it exists to find surfaces
   that say too little, so a generous classifier hides exactly what is being
   looked for.

2. **`innerText` does not contain placeholder text.** The mouth is the
   product's primary surface and scored worst, which was the reason to look
   again rather than write it down. Placeholders are read now. In this case
   the verdict did not change — `Capture inspiration` is two words and would
   not have counted as prose either way — but the walk had been blind to a
   whole channel of copy, and on any surface with a real placeholder it would
   have reported silence over a sentence.

The rule that keeps paying: **when a measurement makes the product look worse
than a person's experience of it, suspect the instrument first.**

## Still unmeasured

- **The first sixty seconds after the first save**, which is where the product
  actually begins: one memory kept, nothing derived yet, no signal, no board.
  A different empty than this one and just as unwalked.
- **A returning account with nothing in it** — someone who signed up, left,
  and came back. The world is the same but the person's expectation is not.
- **The door itself** (`/login`) for someone who has never heard of WAKA.
  `dead-css` walks both its tabs for styling; nothing asks whether it explains
  what the account is for.
