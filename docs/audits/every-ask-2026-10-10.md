## Part 3 — ranked, most important first

Ranked by how hard the founder pushed (repeats, frustration) × how visible it is on screen.

**1. The one-press exit from Talk with WAKA is a dead button.** (#164)
`Menu.tsx:240` looks for an item id ending `"top-of-mind"`; `page-menu.ts` ids are `"me"` and `"agent:<id>"`. The caret and the words "Your top of mind" render on `/chat` and **clicking them does nothing.** The founder reported this exact thing three times — *"I feel like I'm lost how to go back"*, *"I go to my agent and then now I have no way to get back"*, *"I find it so annoying that from talk with Waka, there's no one click to go back"* — and `docs/six-laws.md` calls it "the most-reported law". `Days.tsx:97` carries a splint whose own comment says the control was not working; Chat was never given one. Breaks **Law 2**. Fix: make `Menu` take the exit item explicitly, or give the top-of-mind row a stable id both files agree on, and add the test that would have caught it.

**2. Talk with WAKA is still a page that unmounts the world.** (#143, #167, #149)
*"also dont move talk to waka in a new page. find better transition"* and then, when it hadn't moved, *"Why did you ignore my words that it's confusing that I go into a chat?"* `main.tsx:208` still routes `/chat/:conversationId`; `Home.tsx:15317` and the composer both `navigate()` there. The overlays were built precisely because *"once the world is gone there is nothing to return to"* — and the ruling was applied to saves, principles and the conversation **list** but not to the conversation itself, which is the surface the founder called "the most confusing chat experience ever". Breaks **Law 2** and **Law 3**.

**3. Pressing "my agent" still moves the creature left.** (#122, #133)
`Home.tsx:2355` — `col: -(i + 1)`. Asked three times: *"me is on the left and my agent is on the right"*, *"the agent the character shouldn't move left It should move right"*. Attempted, reverted when the column swap broke 32 room tests, the founder was told, and it was never picked back up. It also undercuts #133 (*"rather than moving rooms it kind of switches itself up to its agent form"*) — the dissolve is cosmetic while the mechanism is still travel. Breaks **Law 1**. The room fixtures that block it are for rooms the founder said to *"get rid of… for now"*; decoupling the test harness from column order is the real fix.

**4. "Capitalize everything" was applied to eight rows and nowhere else.** (#163)
*"Make sure that like everything has the first character of the word capitalized."* 141 lowercase-initial strings still render, including the three claim answers (`yes` / `partly` / `not true`), the bubble's offer (`talk about this`), the form switch (`me` / `my agent`) and the account card (`all settings` / `sign out`) — **all on the home screen the founder was looking at when they said it.** Breaks **Law 3**. This is the single most likely next complaint, because it is the most visible unapplied ruling. It does conflict with the product's quiet lowercase register, so it needs a decision stated once and then applied everywhere, not left half-done.

**5. The new design system has no responsive breakpoints at all.** (#7, #57, #98, #99)
`claims.css` is 80 KB and holds the switcher, both corners, all three overlays, the card grid, the composer, the bubble and the claim ask — and contains **zero width media queries** (only `prefers-reduced-motion`), with 4 `clamp()`. `chat.css` likewise. `home.css` has 14. The founder asked for mobile-first, then *"must be responsive and good front end practices"*, then *"audit this mobile layout"*, then *"audit some mobile experiences… and update my design as well (directly change code)"* — four asks, and the audits were done by reading code because Playwright could not get past the auth wall. Breaks **Law 3** and **Law 6** (a layout never measured is a layout hard-coded).
