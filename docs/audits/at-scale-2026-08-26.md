# The scale audit — the world with a year of memories in it

**Date:** 2026-08-26
**Instrument:** `pnpm e2e:at-scale [--counts 12,200,800]` (`tools/e2e/at-scale.ts`)

## Why this had never been run

Every gate in this repo walks a world holding a handful of things.
`journeys.ts` keeps five. `when-it-fails.ts` keeps five. `first-sixty.ts`
keeps one, on purpose. **The largest field any instrument had ever put on
screen was about a dozen orbs.**

The person this product is for keeps memories for years. A field of five and a
field of five thousand are not the same program — the sim colours, positions
and draws every orb every frame — and nothing had ever asked what that costs.
A product that is delightful at twelve and unusable at eight hundred is a
demo.

Rows are seeded straight into Postgres. Keeping them through the product would
enqueue derivation, and derivation is a model call: 2,500 keeps would be 2,500
calls against the founder's key to answer a question that is not about
derivation. They are also spread across a year of `created_at`, because the
world's clock advances to the newest capture it has seen and the age channel
reads the spread — 2,500 rows sharing one timestamp is a very crowded instant,
not a year of keeping things.

## The numbers

```
     12 memories   pour  550ms   59 fps   worst frame 50ms   heap  5mb   a memory opens
    800 memories   pour  547ms   60 fps   worst frame 33ms   heap  9mb   a memory opens
   2500 memories   pour  548ms   60 fps   worst frame 33ms   heap 10mb   a memory opens
```

**The world scales.** Two hundred times the data costs nothing measurable in
frame rate, five megabytes of heap, and no additional wait. `pour` is
wall-clock from `goto` until the world first says how much it holds; the field
pages in 200s and ten sequential round trips on localhost disappear into the
noise. A memory still opens at every size.

That is the headline, and it is good news. It is worth stating as plainly as a
defect would be.

## The finding: past 2,000, the world quietly holds less than you gave it

```
2500 memories → the world says it holds 2000
```

Not "showing 2,000 of 2,500". Not a note anywhere. The count under the door
reads `2000 inspirations`, the field draws two thousand orbs, and five hundred
memories are absent from both with nothing said.

The ceiling is deliberate and documented, in `lib/data.ts`:

```
FIELD_PAGE      = 200    // the server caps one page here
FIELD_MAX_PAGES = 10     // "the field is honestly short rather than slow"
```

The reasoning is sound — following the cursor forever would let one very old
account stall the world's first paint. **The trade is right; the silence is
not.** "Honestly short" is honest to somebody reading `data.ts`. To the person
it reads as an archive that has quietly lost five hundred things, in a product
whose entire promise is that it keeps what you give it, and whose stated
posture is *no quiet corners*.

This is a year-two bug — it takes roughly two thousand memories to reach — but
for the person this is built for it is not hypothetical, and the failure runs
in the worst direction: the product **understates what it holds** without
saying so. Someone hitting it has every reason to think memories were lost.

**Not fixed here, and the proposal is narrow.** The cap should stay. What is
missing is a sentence, and the count. Two candidates, both cheap:

- the door says `2,000 of 2,500` when the field is short, rather than `2000`;
- or the count comes from a server total and the field says it is showing the
  most recent 2,000 of them.

Either needs the archive's true size, which the list route does not currently
return — so this is a small server change plus one line of copy, and the copy
is the founder's.

### Closed, 2026-08-27 — the first candidate, measured

`GET /api/inspirations` now serves a `total` counted over the query's own
filters and taken **before** the cursor narrows them: the size of the archive
is a fact about the archive, not about a scroll position.
`loadInspirations` carries it up as `{ inspirations, total, short }`, and the
door prints both numbers only when they differ.

Re-run of this same walk, against a live stack and a production build:

```
     12 memories   pour  566ms   60 fps   worst frame 17ms   heap  6mb   a memory opens
   2500 memories   pour  573ms   59 fps   worst frame 33ms   heap 10mb   a memory opens
        └─ short, and says so: "2000 of 2500 inspirations"
```

The pour is unchanged — the count is an index-only scan on
`inspirations_profile_created_idx`, which leads with `profile_id`.

**A room's ground had the same silence one ceiling lower**, at
`GROUP_FIELD_LIMIT = 400`, and is fixed the same way. The count there is the
sensitive half: a room's ground is other people's memories filtered to the ones
they marked public, so a total computed with a predicate even slightly wider
would report the SIZE of what the room is correctly refusing to show. The page
and the count share one named predicate, `memberPublicInspirations`, rather
than two copies — and `room-ground-size.db.test.ts` seeds a room with private
memories and a non-member's memories past the ceiling and demands the total
ignore both.

**What the copy still leaves open.** `2000 of 2500` is the shape, not
necessarily the words, and the words are the founder's. It is one string in
`Home.tsx`.

**Three instruments were wrong before any of this was right**, and all three
were caught by sabotage rather than by review:

- The scale walk itself read the *first* number on the door, which under the
  new sentence is the smaller one — it would have reported the honest
  `2000 of 2500` as the same defect as the silent `2000`. It reads the whole
  sentence now and takes the largest number as what the world claims to hold.
- The room's authorization ratchet counts gates against reads. Extracting the
  shared predicate made it count the predicate's *definition* as a gate,
  leaving exactly one slot of slack — and a deliberately ungated third read of
  layer 1 walked straight through the test written to stop it. A gate that
  counts itself is not a gate.
- Two of the four room-ground cases were seeded with five memories, under a
  ceiling of four hundred, where the loader short-circuits and never runs the
  count at all. Both passed. Both then passed again with the count's predicate
  widened to every member row in the room — the exact leak they existed to
  catch. They seed past four hundred now.

None of the three would have been found by reading the code. Each was found by
breaking the thing the test guards and watching the test stay green.

## What the instrument got wrong first

The first run reported **a pour of 13,092ms — at twelve memories, at two
hundred, at eight hundred and at two and a half thousand, within fifty
milliseconds of each other every time.**

A number that does not move with the load is not measuring the load. Breaking
it down put twelve and a half of those seconds *before* `domcontentloaded`,
with every API call and the canvas landing inside the next 250 milliseconds.

The page preconnects to `fonts.googleapis.com` and `cdn.jsdelivr.net`. On a
machine with no route to either they do not fail fast — they hang. Every other
walk in this repo stubs third-party assets; this file was the only one that
did not.

Reported as written, it would have been a fabricated performance crisis: a
thirteen-second first paint, blamed on a product that pours in about half a
second. The rule that keeps earning its keep — **when a measurement makes the
product look worse than a person's experience of it, suspect the instrument
first** — and its sharper corollary, learned here: *a number that does not
respond to the variable you are changing is not measuring that variable.*

Two smaller ones, same run: the seeder guessed `captured_at` from the client's
`capturedMs` (the column is `created_at`), and the frame sampler was written
as a self-calling arrow, which esbuild's keep-names rewrites into a call to
`__name` that does not exist in the page. It is passed as a string now.
`dead-css.ts` reached that conclusion by the same route; this is the fourth
time in one session, so it is written down in the file.

## Still unmeasured

- **Beyond the ceiling as an experience.** This walk proves the count is
  wrong at 2,500; it does not follow a person who has 10,000 and wants the one
  from March.
- **A slow network, not just a big field.** Ten sequential pages cost nothing
  on localhost. On a phone on mobile data they are ten round trips before the
  count is right.
- **The room and agent walls at scale.** Only the personal wall was filled.
